How I work

Problem first, tools second

Automation is a means, not the goal. This is how I run a product, from problem to result.

01

Discover

Find the real problem first.

I map the flow with the people who run it and measure where time, money and control are lost, before proposing any solution.

02

Define rules and requirements

What lives in heads becomes rules.

I turn what lives in people’s heads into explicit rules and clear requirements, with the exceptions and who decides each one.

03

Prioritise

Customer impact first.

I prioritise by customer impact, financial and operational risk, volume and capacity, and make trade-offs explicit. With management aligned, I accepted that five short-SLA cases would miss their deadline in order to finish the automation that now handles more than 200 rejections a day.

04

Deliver and measure

The result has to last.

I automate only what always follows the same rule, record what changed with the period and sample, and define what happens when something fails.

What I believe

Technology is the means. The result is a better process.

Automation exists to reduce dependency, risk and manual effort.

Every product needs an owner, a direction and a result you can measure.

Well-structured processes make results predictable and sustainable.

Background

From operations to delivery

I understand operations because I started there. At Itaú Unibanco I worked in Customer Due Diligence, notarial guarantees and internal people consulting, always with high-volume processes, data and KPIs.

There I learned to look at the process before the tool.

Today, at a credit fintech, I lead internal products from diagnosis to result: I understand the problem with the people who run it, define the rules, prioritise with management and deliver a working solution. Automation is a means to remove manual dependency and risk from the operation.

My next step is to work as a Product Owner of internal and operational products at fintechs, banks and technology companies, including international and remote teams.
