Case 01Measured from records

How the morning routine stopped depending on manual triggers

Problem
The dialer list had to be ready before 8:00, but each step waited for someone to trigger the previous one.
My part
I measured the waits between steps and set up a sequence that starts on its own at 6:00, with a person monitoring failures.
Result

How I work

Process first, tools second

Automation is one way to solve an operational problem, not the goal. This is how I run an improvement.

  1. 01

    Understand the problem

    I map the flow with the people who run it and measure where time and control are lost, before proposing any solution.

  2. 02

    Write the rules

    I turn what lives in people’s heads into explicit rules, with the exceptions and who decides each one.

  3. 03

    Automate the repetitive

    I automate the steps that always follow the same rule and make clear what stays with a person.

  4. 04

    Measure and sustain

    I record what changed, with the period and sample, document the process and define what happens when something fails.

Another initiative: capacity on peak days

On the days with the largest portfolio intake, processing failed and new customers were left out, exactly on the highest-volume days.

Diagnosis, decision and result

I asked the CRM platform team to investigate the failures at peak times. The performance review showed the cause: contracted capacity was below real demand.

I presented the cause and the business impact to the director, the increase was approved, and I coordinated the change with the technical team on a low-volume day, without disrupting operations.

The infrastructure expertise sat with the platform team. My part was turning the evidence into a business decision. After the change, processing stopped failing on heavy production days.
