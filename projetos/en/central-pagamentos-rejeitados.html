The problem

The same rejected payment could end up in different places, depending on who analysed it.

When a payment is rejected, someone has to decide what to do: issue a new payment slip, redo the operation, refund the customer or investigate. That means comparing the payment gateway statement, the collections system and the status of the agreement with the creditor.

Rejections were copied by hand before any analysis. There were no written rules, so two people could handle the same case differently. Many cases turned into tickets, and the history of each case was scattered outside the workflow.

The hard part is precedence: the same payment can look like a case for a new slip or for a refund, depending on the status of the agreement.

Paulo’s role

He turned operational knowledge into explicit rules and evolved the product with real cases.

The engine started with automatic extraction and four categories: Issue, Redo, Refund and Review. Each time a real case did not fit the rules, Paulo refined the tree, added new channels and created new next steps, until the manual review category was no longer used.

What he led
  • Mapped patterns across cases and the precedence between rules.
  • Designed the decision tree and prioritised its evolution with management and operations.
  • Delivered the automatic extraction, the rules engine and the team’s working panel.
  • Added Jira and email as intake channels for the same engine.
With the teams
  • Validated every rule with the people handling payments day to day.
  • Aligned next steps and exceptions with Operations, CRM and partners.
Out of scope
  • Refunds and proposals are carried out by the responsible teams.
  • The data comes from the payment gateway, the collections system and the creditor.

How it works

Defining the next step is automatic. Executing it depends on the risk.

There are seven next steps today: Redo, Issue, Early settlement, Refund, Automatic Pix refund, Proposal and Write-down in a partner’s CRM.

  1. Intake. Rejections arrive from the collections system (automatic extraction), from Jira and by email, and follow the same rules. automatic
  2. Matching. Each case is compared with the gateway statement and the collections system. automatic
  3. Next step. The decision tree applies precedence and exceptions, sets the next step and records the reason. automatic
  4. Execution. Repetitive next steps, such as redo, are executed by a bot. For early settlement, the operator triggers it with one action and the system reconciles. Refunds and proposals stay with the responsible team. automatic and person

A second payment of the same instalment is routed to the duplicate payment control (case 03).

Result

Every rejection that comes in has a destination, a reason and a single place to work on it.

Analysis and control moved to the panel, which shows the customer’s other payments in the same case. Every decision has its reason recorded, so anyone can understand why a case went where it did.

While investigating a recurring pattern of tickets, Paulo found the rule behind them, tied to the normal posting time of payments, and took it to operations to avoid rework.

Product decisions

Where the engine decides and where a person decides.

  • Any action that moves money starts with a person: refunds and proposals are carried out by the responsible team. human step
  • Every next step records its reason, so the decision can be audited later.
  • New rules come from real cases validated with operations, not from assumptions.
  • Next step: measure rule accuracy and cycle time, from rejection to resolution.

Technical details

Simple tools, well-defined rules.

Technologies, terms and implementation decisionsFor those who want to go deeper

Rules engine: rules written in code that always apply the same decision to the same case and record the reason. It sets the next step; execution depends on the type of action.

Evolution: automatic extraction first; then rules with four categories; then new channels and detailed next steps from real cases. Technically, it went from a Python script with an HTML interface to a Flask application with shared state.
