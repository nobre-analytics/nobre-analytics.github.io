Rejected Payments Decision Engine

A rules engine classifies every rejected payment and points to the next action, with the reason for the decision recorded. Anything without enough evidence goes to review instead of getting a guessed destination.

Business problem

Where each payment went depended on who was on duty.

  • Rejections were copied and pasted by hand before any analysis.
  • With no written rule, two analysts could send the same case to different places.
  • Many cases turned into tickets, and the handling was scattered outside the workflow.

Operating context

Handling a rejection means cross-checking sources and knowing the rules.

Each rejection has to be compared with the gateway statement, the collections system and the status at the creditor. The right rule depends on precedence: the same payment can look like an issue case or a refund case, depending on the status of the agreement.

My responsibility

I mapped the rule with the people who do the work and turned it into a system.

  • I mapped patterns across cases with the people who handled payments every day.
  • I worked out the rules and their precedence.
  • I structured the decision tree and evolved it based on observed cases.
  • I linked each destination to the operational actions that are allowed.
  • I made exceptions go to review, with no destination assigned without evidence.

I built the three parts of the solution: the bot that extracts the rejections, the engine that decides and the panel where the team works.

Analysis and decisions

Start with four categories and add detail only with evidence.

The tree started with four main categories: Issue, Redo, Refund and Review. Between July and October 2026, the rules were split into more specific destinations as cases appeared that the categories did not explain. The most important design decision: when the evidence is not enough, the engine does not pick a destination.

How the solution works

A handling destination is not the same as an automated action.

Automated actionIssuing a payment slip with write-off, and Redo: the bot carries them out and logs every operation.
Action by a personRefunds, contract payoff, proposals and payments still open at the creditor: the engine classifies and the person responsible acts.
ReviewWithout enough evidence for a safe decision, the case goes to human review.
Already resolvedA payment already written off, or a rejection handled in the next run: the case is identified and triggers no new action.
  • Written rules, with the reason for each decision recorded in the case.
  • Write-off bot with a log of every operation.
  • A panel with diagnosis, evidence and action on the same screen, including the customer’s other payments.
  • A second payment for the same installment goes to the duplicate payment controls.

Result and evidence

Case handling moved into the panel, with the customer’s context.

The team’s curation and control now happen inside the panel, which shows the customer’s other payments in the same case. In parallel, I investigated a recurring ticket pattern, identified a business rule related to the normal payment posting timeline, and shared the findings with the operation to reduce rework.

A BackOffice user said the panel is much more practical than Jira, and that seeing the customer’s other payments in the same case made her work a lot easier.

User feedback, Simplic (summarized and translated)

How it was measured. A qualitative result. There is no formal measurement of human time or of rule accuracy, so no performance number is published here.

Limitations

What the engine does not do.

  • There is no formally validated accuracy rate.
  • Destinations that depend on a person are still manual.
  • It does not decide cases without enough evidence.
  • It does not pay refunds: paying the customer back stays with the person responsible.

Sustainability

Someone else can understand and continue the work.

Written rules, a recorded reason on every case and visible exceptions mean the work no longer depends on whoever knows the rule by heart. The rules keep being adjusted based on observed cases.
