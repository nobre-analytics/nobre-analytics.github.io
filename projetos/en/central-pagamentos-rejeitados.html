Rejected Payments Center

The challenge

Where each payment went depended on who was on duty.

  • Rejections were copied and pasted by hand before any analysis.
  • With no written rule, two analysts could send the same case to different places.
  • Every case became a ticket, and the real work got lost in the queue.

My contribution

I mapped the rule with the people who do the work and turned it into a system.

I talked to the people who handled payments every day to understand the real criteria behind each decision. Then I wrote those rules in a deterministic way and built the three parts of the solution: the bot that extracts the rejections, the engine that decides and the panel where the team works.

The approach

Three deliveries in three months, each one built on the last.

  1. Apr 2026First bot

    Automatic extraction of the rejections, which used to be copied by hand.

  2. May 2026Diagnosis engine

    Sixteen deterministic outcomes. The end of manual, on-duty decisions.

  3. Jun 2026Panel in production

    Diagnosis, evidence and action on the same screen.

The solution

Each case arrives already diagnosed, in the queue for the action it needs.

RedoQueue for payments that need to be made again.
ReissueQueue for cases that need a new payment slip.
RefundGoes to the flow that pays the customer back.
ReviewQueue for cases that need another check.

Each queue is independent and has its own automation.

  • Written rules, with the reason for each decision recorded in the case.
  • Write-off bot with a live log of every operation.
  • Payment path dashboard, from rejection to resolution.
  • Focus mode, one case at a time, with JKX shortcuts for next, back and done.

Impact

Work that used to live in tickets now happens in the panel.

The team's curation and control moved into the panel and became automated. One user, who opened Jira tickets every day, no longer needs them.

Mariana, a panel user, said it is much more practical than Jira, and that seeing the customer's other payments in the same case made her work a lot easier.
