Monitoring and reliability

Operations Monitor

A monitor for the operation’s automated routines. On one screen, it shows whether the morning closed, what failed and at which step, and makes it clear when a person needs to act.

Business problem

A failure during the night has to show up before the team starts.

The operation depends on routines that run before the workday. If one of them fails and nobody notices, the team starts the day with incomplete data. The problem was not only running the routines, it was knowing, fast, whether the morning was ready.

Operating context

Some routines are scheduled; others are triggered by hand.

Not every configured routine runs on a schedule: some are triggered on demand and others are turned off. The monitor had to show the real status of each one, without pretending everything was green.

My responsibility

I designed the screen, the watchdog and the investigation of the data issue.

  • I built the screen around three questions managers ask: did the morning close, how much came in, what failed.
  • I set up the health checks, retries and capped automatic restarts.
  • I investigated and fixed the race condition that displayed wrong values.
  • I wrote the operator manual.

Analysis and decisions

When the numbers are wrong, nobody comes back to the panel.

Symptom

Payment and sequence values were sometimes shown wrong.

Root cause

A race condition: two updates wrote the same data with no guaranteed order, and the screen could read a half-written file.

Fix

I made the write atomic, with a lock, so every read finds a complete version.

How the solution works

One screen for the verdict, the detail one click away.

  • Status of each routine, with the time its data was last saved.
  • Failure detection showing the step where a task stopped.
  • Retries and automatic restarts, with a daily cap.
  • Escalation to a person when the retry does not fix it.
  • When a source does not respond, the panel shows "no data" instead of zero.

Result and evidence

Routine failures are visible on one screen, with the step where they stopped.

How it was measured. A qualitative result. The monitor shows the status and failures of each routine, but there is no measurement of time saved compared with manual detection. The states in the demo are simulated and do not prove real runs.

Limitations

What the monitor does not guarantee.

  • It does not replace the check by the person responsible.
  • It does not turn routines that are manual or turned off into scheduled ones.
  • Email alerts are not active in the most recent configuration reviewed.

Sustainability

Controlled recovery and documented knowledge.

Automatic restarts are capped, so a recurring problem is not hidden: after the retries, the case goes to a person. The operator manual explains how to read the panel and what to do for each type of failure. The mobile version takes the morning verdict to managers.

Leadership Principles

How this case connects to Amazon’s principles.

  • Dive DeepI traced the wrong values back to the order of the writes and fixed the cause, not the symptom.
  • Insist on the Highest StandardsThe panel shows "no data" when a source fails, instead of showing zero as if it were real.
  • OwnershipI set a cap on automatic restarts and defined when the problem goes to a person.
