Reconciliation and risk control

How I started identifying duplicate payments before any refund

I built a panel that matches payments in the collections system against the payment gateway statement and organises the cases of customers who paid the same instalment twice. The decision and the refund stay with people.

The problem

Returning money takes proof, not just a suspected duplicate.

Sometimes a customer pays the same instalment twice, for example by Pix and by payment slip. They are owed the extra amount, but it can only be returned after confirming that both credits exist and refer to the same instalment.

This is different from the duplicate write-off reconciler, a separate initiative, which deals with a single payment recorded twice in the collections system. Here there were two real payments.

My contribution

I separated classifying, approving and paying.

The key decision was not to let the tool decide about money. The panel identifies and organises; a person approves the batch; the payment happens outside the tool.

What I did myself
  • Designed the rules that flag possible duplicates from payments and rejections.
  • Built the panel, with a curation mode and an operation mode.
  • Defined the controls that reduce the risk of refunding the wrong payment.
  • Recorded the evidence for each case, so the decision can be checked later.
External dependencies
  • Approving the batch and paying the refund are done by the people responsible.
  • The data comes from the collections system and the payment gateway statement.

How it works

One screen, two roles and a clear status for every case.

  1. Matching. The panel compares the collections system with the gateway statement, for the same customer and instalment. automated
  2. Flagging. With two credits for the same instalment, the original is kept and the second becomes a refund candidate. automated
  3. Review. Anything that does not match goes to review or back to rejected-payment analysis. person
  4. Curation. The decision-maker builds the batch: keeps, flags for review or removes each case. person
  5. Operation. The person carrying it out requests each refund and updates its progress until it is done. person

The status of the agreement decides how the money goes back: if the agreement is settled, the customer gets a refund; if it is active, they can choose between a refund and a deduction from future instalments.

What changed

Each stage is a different status, and only the last one means money returned.

Before, a case depended on separate checks. Now it has its status and history in one place, and the people who decide and the people who execute work on the same screen, with different roles.

Not a refund yet
  1. Identified: the matching found a second credit for the same instalment.
  2. In curation: the decision-maker keeps the case in the batch, flags it for review or removes it.
Carried out by people
  1. Requested: the person carrying it out has requested the refund, and the case is in progress.
  2. Completed: the people responsible made the refund, and the case is closed in the panel.
How the result was assessedQualitative result

The result is qualitative: cases now have their status and history in one place. Identified or in-progress cases are not paid refunds, and there is no verified record of amounts refunded through this flow. That is why no amounts or counts are published.

Limits

What the panel does not do.

  • The panel does not transfer money and does not approve refunds on its own.
  • Deciding on the batch and paying the refund are done by people. human step
  • The matching rule is shown here in simplified form, as a business description.
  • The browser version and the local version have different access setups, so I do not describe a single authentication model.

Tools, terms and implementation decisionsFor readers who want the detail

Matching: a line-by-line comparison of two sources, here the collections system and the gateway statement, to find what appears in both, in only one, or twice.

Curation and operation: two modes of the same screen. In curation, one person decides what goes into the batch; in operation, another person carries it out and records progress.
