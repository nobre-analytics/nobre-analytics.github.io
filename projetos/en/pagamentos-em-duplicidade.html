Reconciliation and risk control

Duplicate Payment Controls

A panel identifies, organizes and records cases where a customer paid the same installment twice. Approving and paying the refund stay with people, outside the tool.

Business problem

Customers who paid twice must get their money back, but only after the duplicate is proven.

Without a match against the statement, the risk goes both ways: refunding a payment that was legitimate or leaving the customer without the money they overpaid.

Operating context

A duplicate payment is not the same as a duplicate write-off.

Here the customer paid the same installment twice. It is a different case from the duplicate write-off reconciler, which handles a payment recorded twice in the collections system. The panel receives the cases flagged for the duplicates workflow.

My responsibility

I designed the identification rules and built the panel.

  • Compare payment and rejection data.
  • Apply rules to find possible duplicates.
  • Organize the cases for human review.
  • Keep controls that reduce the risk of refunding the wrong payment.
  • Record evidence for traceability.

Analysis and decisions

Keep classifying, approving and paying apart.

The main decision was not to let the tool decide about money. The panel identifies and organizes; a person approves the batch; the payment happens outside the tool. The status of the agreement decides how the money goes back:

If the agreement is paid offThe customer gets a direct refund.
If the agreement is still activeThe customer chooses between a refund and a discount on the next installments.

How the solution works

One screen, two roles.

  1. The panel compares payments in the collections system with the gateway statement, for the same customer and the same installment.
  2. When one installment has two credits, the original is kept and the second becomes a refund candidate.
  3. Anything that does not match goes to review or back to the rejections panel.
  4. In curation mode, the person who decides builds the batch; in operation mode, the person who executes requests each refund and tracks progress.

Result and evidence

Each step is a different status, and only the last one means money returned.

  1. Identified: the match finds a second credit for the same installment.
  2. In curation: the person who decides keeps the case in the batch, flags it for review or removes it.
  3. Requested: the person who executes requests the refund, and the case is in progress.
  4. Completed: the refund was paid by the people responsible, and the case is closed in the panel.

How it was measured. A qualitative result: cases now have a status and a history in one place. There is no verified record of amounts actually refunded through this workflow, so no amount or case count is published.

Limitations

What the panel does not do.

  • It does not move money and does not approve refunds on its own.
  • The matching rule is shown here in a simplified business form.
  • The web version and the local version have different access properties, so I do not describe a single authentication model.

Sustainability

Clear responsibilities and a history for every case.

Curation and operation roles stay separate, and each case keeps its status and history. Cases in doubt stay out of the batch until reviewed.

Leadership Principles

How this case connects to Amazon’s principles.

  • Earn TrustI kept classification, approval and payment apart so no money moves because of a decision by the tool.
  • Insist on the Highest StandardsA case only becomes a refund candidate after the statement match; anything that does not match goes to review.
