The challenge

Customers who paid twice must get their money back, but only after the duplicate is proven.

A duplicate can only be refunded after it is confirmed in the payment gateway statement. Without that match, the risk goes both ways: refunding a payment that was legitimate or leaving the customer without the money they overpaid.

How a duplicate is found

The panel cross-checks both sources before any refund.

  1. The panel matches payments in the collections system against the gateway statement, by the customer's CPF (tax ID).
  2. When one installment has two credits, the original stays and the duplicate goes to refund.
  3. Anything that does not match goes to review, or back to the rejections panel.

The refund rule

The status of the agreement decides how the money goes back.

If the agreement is paid offThe customer gets a direct refund.
If the agreement is still activeThe customer chooses: a refund or a discount on the next installments.

The solution

One screen, two roles.

For the person who decides

Curation mode

Decides what goes into the refund batch and flags for review anything still in doubt.

For the person who executes

Operation mode

Requests each refund and marks it as done, with the case history in view.

Governance

Risk control built into the flow.

  • A single sign-in for the FIDC panels.
  • Role-based access: each person sees and does only what their role allows.
  • Audit trail for every decision, with date and reason.
