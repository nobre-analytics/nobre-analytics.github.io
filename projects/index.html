Panels from an automated collections operation

Four screens I designed and built for the daily work of a credit recovery operation: bots that read the systems every morning, an engine that decides what to do with each rejected payment, and the panels where people follow up and act. They are recreated here with made-up data and run in the browser, with no login and no server.

  • 4 working screens
  • Desktop and mobile
  • Light and dark theme
  • HTML, CSS and JavaScript
  1. 01

    Payment rejections

    Each rejected payment arrives already diagnosed and lands in a queue by the decision it needs: redo the agreement, issue a boleto, refund or open a ticket.

    • Dashboard with the payment path, from paid to resolved
    • Simulated write-off bot with a live log
    • Grind mode: one case at a time, with keyboard shortcuts
    Open the screen
  2. 02

    FIDC refunds

    Duplicate payments matched against the gateway statement: the original stays, the duplicate is refunded. Each case moves from pending to requested to completed.

    • Operation mode for who executes, curation mode for who decides
    • Both payments always side by side, with the time between them
    • Cases can be sent back to the rejections panel
    Open the screen
  3. 03

    The Brain, on desktop

    The monitor of the collector bot fleet. It tells you in seconds whether the morning closed, how much came in and what failed, always with the time the data was saved.

    • Collectors, tasks and credit rehabilitation on one screen
    • Collections by channel and by days past due
    • Detail drawer and Ctrl K navigation
    Open the screen
  4. 04

    The Brain, on mobile

    A redesign for managers who check the panel on their phone. The morning verdict comes before the numbers, tables became lists and details slide up in a sheet.

    • First problem visible without scrolling
    • Bottom navigation, within thumb reach
    • Next to the phone: notes on what changed and why
    Open the screen

Brazilian terms on these screens

The operation runs in Brazil, so a few local terms appear in the panels.

Boleto
A bank payment slip, one of the most common ways to pay bills in Brazil.
Pix
Brazil's instant payment system, run by the Central Bank.
CPF
The individual taxpayer ID. Always masked on these screens.
FIDC
A receivables investment fund that buys credit portfolios.
Built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step. Fonts: Bree Serif and Open Sans.
